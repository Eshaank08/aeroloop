The problem

A grounded aircraft costs thousands an hour, and the inspection is done by eye

  • An engine nacelle inspection means a technician walking a surface with a torch, or a drone flown manually by a pilot.
  • Coverage is whatever the operator managed that day. There is no proof the whole surface was seen.
  • The defects that matter most are the ones that need complete, consistent coverage to catch. Southwest 1380 began as a fan blade crack.

Why the software is the hard part

Flying a drone half a metre from a jet engine, in wind, holding a camera steady enough for usable evidence, is a control problem. Every operator, aircraft type and hangar needs different software, and all of it is safety critical.

So the bottleneck is not the airframe. It is writing and certifying the software, over and over.

Why it was not solved, and what we solved

Autonomy was never blocked by intelligence. It was blocked by trust.

What stopped it before

  • You cannot put an agent near a $12M engine if it decides its own success criteria.
  • A model that says "inspection complete" is worth nothing without something independent that can say no.
  • Nobody wants an autonomy layer that keeps flying when it loses contact.

What we built to solve it

  • An adversarial verifier that existed before the agent wrote a line, and grades every mission whatever the agent claims.
  • A safety envelope that bounds every action and hands rejections back to the agent instead of quietly fixing them.
  • Fail closed by default. Lose the agent and the drone returns home. The mission can never report PASS.
It caught the agent twice. Devin claimed a mission complete with 3 of 9 views captured; the verifier returned 33% against a 95% bar and refused it. That refusal is the product. Not the flying.
Humans keep four authorities: authorise, confirm identity, intervene, sign off3 / 5
How we solved it with Devin

We did not train a model. We used an agent harness as the autonomy layer.

A model endpoint returns text. A harness comes with persistent sessions, its own shell, editor, tests and version control, and the judgement to use them. That difference is the whole project.

An API could not do this

Devin found a ZeroDivisionError in the flight controller it had written, reproduced it, derived a fix, proved the routing was unchanged over 2000 random cases, added a regression test and opened a pull request. Thirteen of its PRs are merged. No text completion does that.

One harness, both jobs

The same harness is the engineer that writes and repairs controller2.py, and the mission agent that chooses every inspection action in flight. One resumable session per mission, so later decisions reflect what earlier ones revealed.

Why a harness is the right bet

No training data, no eval framework, no agent scaffolding to maintain. Our engineering goes into the verifier and the safety envelope, which is where aviation rigour belongs. The harness is the part we should not be building ourselves.

The boundary is a typed action contract, not a prompt. Devin v3 sits behind it today; a stronger version drops in without touching the simulator, the envelope or the verifier. The same is true in reverse: swap our controller for a certified autopilot and the autonomy layer is unchanged.

What we did, and what is next

A judge types a sentence. The drone flies. Something independent grades it.

Working today

  • Plain English in: "inspect the underside on a random scene." The area is authorised locally, then Devin chooses every action.
  • A rate-controlled quadrotor simulator where a view only counts within 0.5 m, aimed within 60°, and steady.
  • Seeded wind and gusts the agent is never shown, only sensed.
  • Every observation and decision hashed into a signed, replayable artifact.

Next, in order

  • Real perception: camera, audio and thermal into the same observation contract.
  • Swap our controller for a certified autopilot over MAVLink. The mission layer does not change.
  • Independent hardware emergency stop, outside every software authority.
  • Cage trial on a real component, then inspector-led validation with a regulator in the room.
Everything here is simulated, and it verifies inspection coverage rather than detecting defects. We would rather tell you that than have you find it in the demo.
